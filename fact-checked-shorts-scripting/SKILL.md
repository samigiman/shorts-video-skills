# Fact-checked shorts scripting

A short lives or dies in its first two seconds, and a science channel lives or dies on whether viewers trust it. This skill is about getting both: hooks that stop the scroll, and scripts where every claim survives a viewer with Google.

## 1. Ideas and titles

- Offer several topics at once, grouped (local/national angle, current tech, body and mind, provocative), each with a ready hook. Mark your 2–3 picks and say why in a few words.
- Local angles travel: a video tied to the viewer's own country or city ("the first fibre cable under the Caspian went live this month") beats a generic one. But a topic is only local if the content is; don't paste a city name onto a generic fact.
- Current events beat evergreen ones when they exist. Search for news from the last weeks before writing about any technology, company or record — the user may know something new (e.g. a company rewriting its app) that your knowledge lacks. When a user points out a fresh fact, verify it and work it in.
- Expect some ideas to be rejected flatly. Drop them without argument and continue with the rest.

## 2. Hooks

Good hooks are provocative, specific and true:
- A teasing claim the video then earns: "Wi-Fi doesn't stand for anything."
- A familiar saying put to the test: "Is an atheist only an atheist until the plane starts falling? Science tested it."
- A pun or wordplay in the audience's language works well when the topic allows it.

Rules:
- The hook must not overclaim what the video says later. "Every atheist prays at 10,000 m" sounds stronger, but if the video then has to say "not everyone", the hook lied. Prefer the strong version that stays true.
- In a reply video, the hook must not repeat the viewer's question; the question comes after the follow call.
- When asked for "more provocative", give 3–4 options, mark one, and note any option that would contradict the content.

## 3. Script shape (~60–90 s, ~150–220 words)

1. Hook (1–3 short sentences)
2. Follow call (one line, e.g. "Follow us to support the channel!")
3. Reply videos only: "A viewer asks: … Let's see." (leave ~4 s of empty screen centre for the comment sticker)
4. 3–5 beats of explanation, each one concrete (a number, an experiment, a mechanism)
5. One honest caveat where it exists ("the exact order is still debated")
6. One-line takeaway, then a question to the audience + comment call

Write numbers as words for TTS; captions convert them back. Use the proper scientific term in the target language.

## 4. Verify everything that can be checked

- Search before writing for every fact describing the world now (who, what, how many, latest version) and every number. Prefer primary sources: the paper, the official blog, the agency release, an encyclopedia for stable facts.
- Keep the sources for the delivery message.
- If two sources disagree, use the claim both support (or the more conservative one) and note it.
- If you cannot find a source, either cut the claim or say "reportedly". Never invent a figure for a graphic (e.g. a made-up temperature drop); show the qualitative idea instead.
- Distinguish hypotheses from findings in the script itself ("one hypothesis, still unproven, is…").

## 5. Sensitive topics (religion, politics, identity)

- Science neither proves nor disproves God. Say so when a video touches belief, and teach the science (psychology, history, biology) without taking a side.
- Never mock believers, non-believers or commenters. A comment that challenges the channel is content, not an enemy.
- Avoid teleological wording in evolution topics ("the problem → the solution", "decided to grow an organ"). Use "variation + selection", "those that happened to… survived".
- Give the creator a short, friendly pinned-comment text for heated threads: concede what the viewer got right, restate the neutral point, one emoji at most.

## 6. Checking a viewer's comment

1. State the actual claim charitably (viewers often merge two things).
2. Research it; lead with a one-line verdict: right / partly right / wrong.
3. "Where they're right" and "where it doesn't hold", each point sourced.
4. Check the channel's own video against the finding; if the video was imprecise, say so plainly and give better wording.
5. A 2–4 sentence reply the creator can paste. Offer a reply video with a hook when the topic is strong.

## 7. Posting kit (with each finished video)

- Structure with rough timestamps
- Verified facts with citations; simplifications and unverified points, each in one line
- Production cost (e.g. TTS credits) and any files left on the user's computer
- A caption (1–2 lines, one emoji) + 5–7 hashtags mixing topic, channel and reach tags

Keep the delivery message about one phone screen; the user reads on mobile.
